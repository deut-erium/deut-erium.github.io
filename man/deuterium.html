NAME

deuterium — security blog, occasionally self-aware

SYNOPSIS

deuterium [--skin=SKIN] [--no-buttons] [POST…]

DESCRIPTION

deuterium reads, solves, and writes up. The blog ships CTF writeups with the wrong turns included, cryptography tutorials, and essays that were definitely not written at 3am. Every page is static, local, and faster than your reflexes.

The interface is a lie in the friendly direction: the skins are not themes, they are moods. There are 48 of them. The dice does not take responsibility.

OPTIONS

--skin=SKIN
Wear one of 48 identities. Append ?skin= to any URL, or roll the dice and live dangerously. The choice is remembered, like most mistakes.
--no-buttons
Unsupported. See BUGS.
-v, --verbose
No-op. Everything is already verbose.
-h, --help
You are reading it.

GAME CENTER

Some posts carry challenge boxes. Flags are checked in your browser; nothing is sent anywhere. The scoreboard lives in your localStorage and judges silently.

EXIT STATUS

ENVIRONMENT

HOME
You are here.

FILES

~/.deuterium/progress
Kept in your browser. We never see it, by design and by laziness.

SECURITY CONSIDERATIONS

Zero cookies. This is not a promise on a banner; it is an assertion in CI. If a cookie ever appears, the build fails and the banner becomes a lie, which is worse than a cookie.

BUGS

  1. Buttons may be pressed. Consequences unknown.
  2. The dice is not cryptographically secure. Do not derive keys from it.
  3. 3am prose ships as-is.
  4. Some skins are loud. This is a feature wearing a bug costume.
  5. If you find a real one, the security policy accepts mail.

STANDARDS

Implements no known standard. Aspires to RFC 2324.

AUTHOR

Written by Himanshu Sheoran. Complaints may be directed to the teapot.
