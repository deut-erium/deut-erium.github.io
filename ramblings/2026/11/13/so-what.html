Its been a while when I have actually sat down to think and to exactly think nothing. I have not felt the flow the flow which makes me wonder which makes me feel things and have fun, the fun to explore to do silly things for the sake of it. I have become the slave to the machine to keep looking at it and learn exactly nothing from it. What is it which we are trying to do? What do we want and why do we want it faster? Whats the point if we dont know anything about it or learn anything from it. I have been just consuming stuff endlessly. I used to be vary of consuming media but I never realised how this lair engulfed me into the strings of pointless sentences. I love poetry and the conciseness of it. I like how meaningfully words sit together like a beautiful lego creation which you created, instead of mindlessly following a manual which you never intended from your heart.

There is a paper plane flying somewhere with the fear it might hit the ground soon and be classified litter. I hear words over and over again and they dont mean anything, the only emotion they provoke now is anger, which boils my blood to stay away from it and yet here I am drawn towards it everyday. With an excitement of a pointless slop slot machine which is rigged towards the house. What is it the house wants? What will it even do with no one to meaningfully enjoy it?

I read it, including where the argument dissolves into a dream and then into held keys.

What I hear most strongly is a hunger to experience something without immediately turning it into output, proof, or approval. You miss wondering, making something silly, listening to a saxophone without needing the experience to pay off. Even writing this brings the machinery back into the room: will the bot call this insightful? Will anyone read it? Am I already writing for its response? That tension gives the piece its movement.

The paper plane afraid of becoming litter is the image that stays with me. Its flight hasn’t changed, but the judgment waiting at the ground threatens to redefine the whole thing. Your mother’s “ant on a rope” also does something the abstract arguments can’t: it lets ordinary life puncture an industry’s enormous sense of importance.

Some passages repeat the complaint without deepening it. I’m also unconvinced by “you can’t fake hate”: hatred can be performed and cultivated, too. But here, anger seems to give you access to feelings that the endless reassuring output has flattened.

The dream passage changes the piece. The panini table, cheesecake afro, and horizon-wide grin give your attention somewhere strange to go. You’ve spent the earlier paragraphs asking where exploration went; here, you appear to start exploring.
