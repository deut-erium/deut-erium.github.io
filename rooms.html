Escape room

Three locked doors, one constraint solver. Each room is an honest SMT instance handed to the real Z3 compiled to WebAssembly and served from this site; each satisfying model opens its door and drops one letter of the exit code. Nothing engine-shaped loads until you press a Solve button, and nothing ever leaves the page. The harness is the same one that drives the playground.

Room 1 — The schedule

Three speakers (Ada, Bo, Cy), three slots, two rooms (a, b). Every speaker talks exactly once, no room hosts two talks in the same slot, every slot gets a talk, Cy is unavailable in slot 2, and the opening talk is Ada in slot 1, room a. Ask Z3 for any schedule that satisfies all of it.

How the room works

    
Each Solve press builds a fresh SMT-LIB encoding (the one behind
      See the constraints), wakes the engine, and checks it under a
      30-second client-side budget. The first press pulls ~35 MB of WASM
      from this site; if the host does not send COOP/COEP, the page registers
      a small isolating service worker under /rooms/, reloads
      once to unlock the off-thread solver, and resumes your room. After
      that the engine is warm and every room is a few hundred milliseconds.

    
The rooms: scheduling as distinct integer cells, factoring as
      nonlinear arithmetic over a leaked high-bit window, and liar logic as
      boolean biconditionals. The exit flourish dispatches a
      deuterium:solved event with id
      escape-room, so the challenge scoreboard picks the room
      up like any other cleared challenge. Reduced-motion visitors get the
      same verdicts with no animation.
